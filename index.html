What I build

Somewhere to climb, hide, and land.

Not kits, not furniture you assemble. Everything gets framed into the room — anchored to real structure, cut to your walls, finished to match the house.

Climbing walls

Plywood face, real holds, bolted straight through. Routes move around as they get taller and braver.

Rope nets

Knotted nets and rope ladders. A second way up, and the way most kids actually come back down.

Monkey bars

A proper run overhead, mounted into the ceiling framing and set at a height they can actually reach. Ends over something soft.

Forts & lofts

Platforms and hideouts sized to your actual ceiling — not to a photo from a house with twelve-foot walls.

Reading nooks

Bench, bookshelf, warm light and a curtain they can pull shut. Every room needs a quiet half.

My basement

This is the one that started it.

Low ceiling, block walls, laundry in the corner — the same basement most houses around here have. It took a handful of weekends.

The mural, the string lights, the curtain on the nook: none of that was on the drawing. That part happened once the framing was up and we could stand in it and see what it wanted to be.

That's why I only take one room at a time. The building is the easy half.

And a concept

So you can see it before it exists.

Alongside the measured drawing you get a coloured concept of the room — palette, finishes, where everything lands. It's the easiest way to change your mind while changing your mind is still free.

The boring important part

Built like there's a kid on it.

Because there will be. I build to the published playground safety guidance, adapted for indoors and for the ages of the kids using the room. None of it is an upgrade — it's just how it gets built.

Anchored

Everything ties into real framing, not drywall. Hardware goes through the plywood and gets a nut on the back.

Gaps

Openings are sized so nothing can catch a head or an arm — the thing that matters most and that nobody thinks about.

Landings

Rated matting and foam under everything they can climb or hang from. Not an upgrade, just how it gets built.

Materials

Low-VOC finishes and formaldehyde-free plywood. It's a room they'll be in every day.
